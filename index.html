I’m Saurabh Ranjan, a cognitive neuroscientist. My dissertation, Reality Monitoring in Humans and Artificial Intelligence, found that humans and LLMs share the same core capability that fails during hallucination: telling what you generated yourself from what came from somewhere else. I tested this with behavioral experiments, neuroimaging (UK Biobank, MEG/EEG), and matched human–LLM benchmarking pipelines.

I'm currently a Researcher in AI for Biomedical Health Outcomes at UF, co-developing UF's Master's curriculum on Data Science and Agentic LLMs.

I previously worked with Dr. Brian Odegaard (blue sky, twitter\x) at PACLAB, and with Prof. Narayanan Srinivasan (now at IIT Kanpur) at CBCS.

My publications are on Google Scholar, and my open-source work (agentic LLM tooling, cognitive modeling) is on GitHub.

Highlighted Projects

Mind & Cognition

Reality Monitoring with Humans and LLMs

  • Discovered that humans and LLMs share a core "reality monitoring" capability (telling self-generated from externally-generated information) that fails during hallucination in both.
  • Ran behavioral experiments with 100+ human participants matched against LLM simulations, producing standardized human–LLM benchmarking pipelines.
  • Identified a dissociation between single-trial and conversation-memory regimes in LLMs that inverts source-attribution accuracy, with direct implications for how clinical AI assistants should manage chart history and cited evidence.
  • Published in Neuroscience Research (2024) and presented at the Psychonomic Society and APS annual meetings.
  • Currently extending this into a benchmark for "information generation awareness" in frontier LLMs (working paper).

GitHub: LLM-RM · GitHub: predict_perception_imagination_tgm · Papers

Human Imagination

  • Proposed a new computational model of visual imagination that reframes the heterarchy-vs-hierarchy debate in brain theory (Physics of Life Reviews, 2024).
  • Built and compared imagination networks across Florida, London, and Poland cohorts using psychological network analysis.
  • Extended the same network-analysis approach to compare population-level cognitive structure between humans and synthesized LLM populations.
  • Published "Offline World Models as Imagination Networks in Cognitive Agents" (arXiv:2510.04391, 2025). The paper argues world models are the AI analogue of human imagination.
  • Methodology generalizes to subgroup discovery in health and behavioral data, directly applicable to biomedical AI work.

GitHub: imagine_world_model · Preprint (arXiv)

Education and Cognition

  • Investigated how undergraduate students reason about ethical and algorithmic decision-making, an early study of human trust in automated decision-making (ACM SIGCSE, 2022).
  • Co-designed "touchy-feely" embodied digital affordances that improved students' epistemic access to abstract science concepts.
  • Studied embodied-interaction technology design to support model-based reasoning in developing-country classrooms.
  • Produced behavioral analyses for an embodied-cognition educational technology intervention at TIFR, Mumbai.
  • Published across IEEE T4E, ACM SIGCSE, and the Journal of Computer Assisted Learning.

Papers

Sense of Agency

  • Investigated the sense of agency for future-directed intentions using the intentional binding paradigm.
  • Showed intentional binding effects extend to intermediate outcomes, not just final ones, evidence for graded, forward-looking self-monitoring.
  • Led the full research lifecycle solo: hypothesis, design, data collection, and analysis.
  • Shared as an OSF preprint and presented at the Annual Conference of Cognitive Science, IIT-Guwahati (2018).
  • Foundational to later work on self-monitoring and control: the same construct now central to agentic AI systems that plan and act autonomously.

Preprint (OSF)

Psych Scanner

  • Built a production-grade Python framework (LangChain + LangGraph + Pydantic) for running cognitive experiments with LLMs at scale.
  • Automates and scales "LLMs as a participant" studies, replacing ad hoc scripts with a reusable, tested pipeline.
  • Supports persona management, conversation-history manipulation, and population-level synthesis out of the box.
  • Submitted as a standalone methods paper: "Psych Scanner: A Framework for Systematic Cognitive Evaluation of Large Language Models."
  • Open-source and actively maintained, with new commits every month in 2026.

GitHub: psychscanner

MetaSignal

  • A Python package implementing standard Signal Detection Theory metrics (d′, criterion) and metacognitive measures (meta-d′) for confidence/accuracy research.
  • Replaces the outdated MATLAB toolboxes most labs still use for meta-d′ estimation with a modern, reusable Python package.
  • Built to support reality-monitoring and metacognition studies across the Odegaard Lab.
  • Most actively developed repo in 2026: the computational backbone behind ongoing lab analyses.
  • Open-source and reused across multiple published papers.

GitHub: metasignal
